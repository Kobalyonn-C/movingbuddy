Moving Box Calculator: how many boxes do you need?

Tell us about your home and what you own, and MovingBuddy estimates the boxes and packing materials for your move. Every number is editable, so you can adjust it to your real belongings.

How the estimate works

Each belongings category (clothes, books, kitchen items and so on) starts with a base number of boxes that grows with the number of people, bedrooms and floor area. That number is then multiplied by your amount of belongings: 0.75 for minimalist, 1 for average and 1.3 for a lot. Each category is split across box sizes using a fixed mix, for example books mostly go in small boxes because they are heavy, while bedding goes mostly in large ones.

Choosing box sizes

Small boxes suit books, cans and anything dense. Medium boxes are the all-rounder for kitchenware, electronics and decorations. Large boxes are for light, bulky things such as pillows and comforters. Wardrobe boxes let you move hanging clothes without folding them.

Packing tips

  • Keep each box under about 15 kg so it can be lifted safely.
  • Label every box with its room and a short description.
  • Pack a separate essentials box for your first night.
  • Order about 10% extra boxes. Running out on packing day is the most common problem.

Assumptions: a roll of tape for about 12 boxes, 0.3 kg of packing paper and 1.5 m of bubble wrap per box. These are rules of thumb, not measurements.

Worked example: a two-bedroom home

With the default inputs (2 bedrooms, 2 people, 70 m² or about 753 sq ft, an average amount of belongings and a kitchen) the calculator returns 49 boxes: 15 small, 20 medium, 11 large and 3 wardrobe boxes. It also lists 5 rolls of tape, 15 kg (about 33 lb) of packing paper and 74 m (about 243 ft) of bubble wrap.

Entering your home size in square feet

The property size field uses square meters. Divide your square footage by 10.764 to convert it, so an 800 sq ft apartment is about 74 m².

When to adjust the numbers

  • If you own a lot of books, raise the Books quantity. They go mostly into small boxes, so they add boxes quickly.
  • Ticking Garage, Storage room or Garden adds boxes to the Miscellaneous category.
  • Unticking Kitchen reduces the kitchen estimate to a fraction of the full amount, which suits homes that barely cook.

What to do with your result

Once you have a box count, open the Moving Volume Calculator to see how much space those boxes take alongside your furniture, and use the Moving Cost Calculator to price the boxes and packing materials. The Moving Checklist schedules box collection about four weeks before moving day.

Frequently asked questions

How many boxes does a two-bedroom home need?

With two people and an average amount of belongings, this calculator typically gives roughly 50 boxes. Your own result depends on what you own, so adjust the quantities.

Should I buy or borrow boxes?

Borrowing or reusing saves money, but new boxes are stronger and uniform in size, which makes them easier to stack and load.

Are the results exact?

No. They are estimates based on documented rules of thumb. Use the editable quantities to match your belongings.

How many boxes do I need for a studio or one-bedroom apartment?

For one bedroom, one person, 45 m² (about 484 sq ft) and an average amount of belongings, the calculator gives 32 boxes. Treat that as a starting point and edit the category quantities to match what you own.

Can I enter square feet?

The size field is in square meters. Divide square feet by 10.764 before entering it.

Does the calculator count dish packs or other specialty boxes?

No. It estimates four sizes: small, medium, large and wardrobe. Fragile kitchenware is included in the kitchen category, so add specialty boxes yourself if you plan to buy them.
