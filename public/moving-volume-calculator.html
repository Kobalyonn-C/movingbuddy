Moving Volume Calculator: how many cubic meters are you moving?

Volume decides which vehicle you need and how long loading takes. Pick your furniture and appliances, add anything unusual with its dimensions, and see your total in cubic meters.

What the numbers mean

Each item has a typical volume in cubic meters (m³), based on common sizes and including the space it takes up when loaded. A double bed, for example, is counted at 2 m³ because the frame, slats and mattress rarely stack tightly. Your actual furniture may be larger or smaller, which is why you can add custom items using length, width and height in centimeters. The formula is length × width × height ÷ 1,000,000.

The packing allowance

Items never fit together perfectly. The allowance adds a percentage (15% by default) to cover gaps, awkward shapes and padding. Increase it for lots of fragile or irregular items, or reduce it if you will load carefully.

Using your result

As a rough guide, a studio is often a few cubic meters and a family home is often several times that, but there is no substitute for counting your own items. Once you have a total, the Truck Size Calculator uses it automatically.

Worked example: a one-bedroom load

Suppose you are moving one sofa, two armchairs, a double bed, a wardrobe, a refrigerator, a washing machine, a dining table with four chairs and 40 boxes. That is 52 items totaling 13.4 m³ (about 473 cubic feet). With the default 15% allowance the result is 15.41 m³ (about 544 cubic feet). By category: living room 3.6 m³, bedroom 3.8 m³, kitchen 3.2 m³ and other 2.8 m³.

Cubic meters and cubic feet

The calculator works in cubic meters. One cubic meter is about 35.31 cubic feet, so multiply your result by 35.31 to compare it with a rental company's cubic-foot figures. For custom items the dimension fields are in centimeters. Multiply inches by 2.54 to convert.

Measuring a custom item

Measure the longest, widest and tallest points, including legs or handles that stick out. Enter a quantity if you have several identical pieces.

Where the volume goes next

Your total is saved in this browser and used automatically by the Moving Truck Size Calculator, which turns it into a vehicle and a number of trips. If you have not counted boxes yet, start with the Moving Box Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does the volume include boxes?

Yes, if you enter a quantity for Boxes. If you used the Box Calculator first you can copy that number across with one click.

What allowance should I use?

15% suits most moves. Use 20-25% for many awkward items and 5-10% for neatly stackable loads.

How do I add an item that is not listed?

Use the custom item form with its dimensions and quantity.

How many cubic feet is my result?

Multiply the cubic meters by 35.31. For example, 15.41 m³ is about 544 cubic feet.

Can I enter dimensions in inches?

The custom item form uses centimeters. Multiply inches by 2.54 first.

Is volume the same as weight?

No. Volume tells you how much space a load needs. Weight limits are separate, so check the payload of any vehicle you rent.
